Rules as nudges

A rule becomes a nudge to whichever agent runs it: the writer, the review lead, the Lead, or whoever writes or reviews a rule. It says what it noticed, where, which standard makes it a concern, how sure it is, and where else to read. The agent reads those places and makes the call. A rule that can't settle its standard from what it collected still nudges, with the claim check ("worth checking"), and never fails a run.

Each rule names its chunk: the evidence its judgment needs, such as a section's own text with the files it loads. The engine already collects most chunks. What it can't collect yet, and the nudge's report fields, go to an engine change in sunstack-tools. The skills that change are skill-rules, domain-decision-models, decision-model-review, skill-creation, skill-review, skill-design, skill-orchestrator, skill-audit and domain-skill-crafting, with its rules folder. All of them teach their part on one worked example: #187's AgentStudio line.

What "related locations" covers, and what proves the whole thing worked, carry provisional answers in the requirements until you say otherwise. Whether an agent acting on a nudge makes the right call waits for evals. The design review hasn't run yet.
