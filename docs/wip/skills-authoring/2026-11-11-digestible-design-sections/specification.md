# Proposed authoring contract

1. Before writing a substantive section, name the reader question and the source-supported relationship needed to answer it; mandatory engineering fields constrain correctness rather than prescribe the page's headings.
2. Compose the section's explanation and diagram together: the text introduces context and interprets consequences; the diagram exposes the relevant owners, movement, alternatives, state or failure relationship. A paragraph in a box does not satisfy that job.
3. Keep the decisive relationship and its interpretation readable together at the intended reading size; if a view hides its important outcomes, revise its supported form, direction, labels, size or decomposition rather than treating valid markup as acceptance.
4. Introduce a technical term through the action or distinction the reader needs before relying on it; a cold reader's interpretation gap is evidence to inspect, not automatic proof of a missing feature.
5. Put exact supporting fields, references and derivations below the explanation or behind useful labels, while keeping governing guards, thresholds, important failures, risks and unknowns visible. Agents can inspect every required field in the same authoritative HTML source.
6. Preserve the four semantic owners: Requirements explains authorized needs and user experience; Specification explains entities and observable rules; Program Design explains realization and ownership; Verification explains the deciding observation and its limits. Do not copy one diagram or normative rule across all four as their layout.
7. Keep stable U/E/R and other existing identities within meaningful sections; one section may explain several related identities. Reorganizing reading order changes no authority, accepted coverage or required trace rows.
8. Apply the shared presentation owner's Map and conditional Walkthrough teaching to actual design explanations. A group reaches context; a need reaches its text; related rule/design/proof links reach their homes. Never add a fixed four-stage branch or route merely to fill a pattern.
9. A tiny independent fact or metadata row does not become an artificial section needing a manufactured diagram. Split only when the reader question changes; related views and interpretation remain together.
10. Use the existing author/self-review and commissioned review paths to inspect the actual affected rendered section and reconstruct its meaning. Static, source-only, reconstructed-reading and owner-acceptance results stay separate; required but unavailable inspection is a named gap.
11. Reuse semantically current evidence and inspect only affected views; this adds no blanket browser matrix, word limit, component count or compulsory new reviewer per edit.

These obligations refine presentation, not product behavior. They do not waive required semantic fields or require physical one-file-per-question decomposition. Source previews and published bindings retain their existing trust, preparation and delivery constraints.
