# Observable obligations

- R1: A substantial work account groups the work into meaningful subjects whose questions, reasons, actual agent actions, changes, outcomes and remaining limits can be followed without reading its archive.
- R2: Each substantive explanatory section composes written explanation with a diagram of its relevant source-supported relationships, keeping the two adjacent and complementary.
- R3: A chunk answers one coherent reader question and gives enough context to interpret its diagram, rather than compressing unexplained technical nouns into labels or splitting each stored row into its own item.
- R4: A substantial account’s Map connects reader concerns or work subjects to their related explanatory locations, with meaningful labels/descriptions and stable real targets, instead of duplicating the heading outline.
- R5: A Walkthrough is added when an ordered question or journey needs several existing chunks and its narration directs attention to those targets rather than duplicating their content or merely stepping through all headings.
- R6: Rich components expose the point of the content, while detail and evidence remain available without overwhelming the main explanation or hiding material failure conditions.
- R7: Claims about actual work retain writer attribution, authority, target and proof limits, with source evidence addressable and the complete original capture available separately.
- R8: Simple facts, one-line empty statuses and content without supplied relationships remain concise rather than acquiring fabricated relationships to fill a template.

| R1–R3 | A deployment investigation explains a retry decision with participants, failure and change beside its narrative. | A training-pipeline report places every event in a diagram or repeats the same paragraph inside every box. | Both cases preserve meaningful question/relationship selection; passes. |
| R4 | A mobile migration account lets “keep offline work” reach storage, conflict handling and rehearsal evidence across chunks. | A security audit Map copies headings and provides no concern-to-evidence connection. | Semantic relationship, not product schema; passes. |
| R5 | A recovery account traces whether a successful dry run justifies cutover across constraints, rehearsal and approval. | A release changelog receives a guided tour through every heading despite no ordered reader question. | Conditional narrative need distinguishes them; passes. |
| R6–R8 | A permission review needs attributed narrative observations, a boundary diagram and an unresolved authority caveat; no code is supplied. | A passing fixture report is promoted to production success or an ordinary “no decision pending” line gets invented arrows. | Source and meaning constrain richness; passes. |

These obligations describe composition, not a new output schema or a prescribed count/layout. The existing four work-log h2 questions remain; substantive h3 chunks carry explanations and diagrams. A heading with only a short empty-status answer is not a substantive explanatory chunk.

R9: The logger’s record reference must describe historical Story rows and their supersedes links for reading, without directing a page renderer to create or refresh them. Preserve all ten recorded kind meanings, field names, immutable history and existing closure semantics. Transfer: an incident-page renderer can interpret old Story backing without writing a new Story; an independently commissioned material engineering decision still uses the unchanged decision-record procedure. Bad choice: rewriting a summary merely because a requested page needs newer prose.
