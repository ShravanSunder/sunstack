# Working with the Implementer

This is your side of commissioning, correcting and closing out an Implementer. `practices-manage-agents` owns its form, model, route and packet. Return the write scope, stop condition and paired Verifier you gave it, then your disposition of its handback.

## Commission

An Implementer always works beside a paired Verifier. An owner override of the Implementer's form changes how it runs and leaves the pairing in place. If the packet can't name the pair, for example because a native Worker has no Router address, keep the Verifier, record how the two reach each other for this run, and bring the missing contract field to the owner as a gap.

Give it the write scope, meaning the exact paths it may change. Give it a stop condition that says when to hand back early, including a result that contradicts the plan. Name the paired Verifier and how to reach it. A design choice the slice depends on goes into the plan before the commission goes out.

Its own product tests and proof stay visible to it, because it builds against them. The cases and checks of a held-out evaluation stay out of everything it reads, the plan included. `working-with-verifier.md` owns that rule.

When the job is writing skills, the owner's direction applies: "especially if they are writing the skill then inventory for slop and infractions". Ask for that inventory in the commission, and name what it walks against: `shared-references/humanizer.md` as the slop catalog, read as a list of tells and never called from the skill it writes, and the self-check list from the skill-authoring system the job uses. It returns each item fixed, or kept with a reason.

**Case:** the owner directed "use opus 5.5 worker with all the skills to write instead of a sidekick". The Verifier's packet needs its Implementer's Router address, and a native Worker has none, so the Verifier was dropped at first. Nobody had waived the pairing. The form override was read as a waiver because the packet couldn't express the pair. **Clean case:** the corrected plan kept one Verifier Sidekick on Sol, a different lineage from the Opus writers, paired with the whole Worker workstream. The Lead relayed between them, and the contract gap went to the owner as an open item.

## Handback

Read the diff before the summary. Check every changed path against the write scope; a file outside it needs its own disposition even when the change helps. Then hold the diff against the plan slice, as the main skill's case shows.

For skill writing, spot-check the inventory. Open two or three changed sentences, check them against the tells yourself, and send any miss back with its anchor.

Send each correction to the same Implementer, with what changed and the anchor it rests on. A new Implementer has to rebuild the context and can bring back the gap you just closed.

## When to stop

The relationship closes when the diff sits inside the write scope and matches the plan slice, the inventory has every item fixed or kept with a reason, and the paired Verifier's observations are in. Acceptance then follows the main skill. Stop sending corrections when the next one would change design meaning, scope or a contract; that goes back to the plan or to the owner.
