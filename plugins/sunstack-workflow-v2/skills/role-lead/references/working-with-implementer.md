# Working with the Implementer

This is your side of commissioning, correcting and closing out an Implementer. `practices-manage-agents` owns its form, model, route and packet. Return the write scope, stop condition and paired Verifier you gave it, then your disposition of its handback.

## Commission

Select existing governing assignment/plan/procedure files that establish the inputs below, then send only role/files for this role. The recipient reads its own preamble and resolves the rich context; these input obligations do not require an inline packet or a newly recreated packet file. Continuations and corrections use the same two-field content with current governing correction sources.

The Implementer is always a Sidekick, paired with the workstream's Verifier Sidekick. Native coding subagents are its helpers, not alternative Implementer seats. Establish the workstream, dependencies, sequencing, scope, write grant and completion/stop conditions in governing sources, plus the pair's real address. Leave execution steps and helper count/decomposition to the Implementer within the approved model/access context; no per-child Lead approval, trace or Verifier pair is required.

The Implementer investigates and repairs ordinary defects within that boundary. A changed governing contract, expected outcome, ownership, proof obligation or unavailable authority comes back with evidence; a missing cosmetic packet detail does not stop unrelated authorized work.

Its own product tests and proof stay visible to it, because it builds against them. The cases and checks of a held-out evaluation stay out of everything it reads, the plan included. `working-with-checker-verification.md` owns that rule.

When the job is writing skills, the owner's direction applies: "especially if they are writing the skill then inventory for slop and infractions". Name that inventory in the governing assignment source, with what it walks against: `sunstack-dev/shared-references/humanizer.md` as the slop catalog, read as a list of tells and never called from the skill it writes, and the self-check list from the skill-authoring system the job uses. It returns each item fixed, or kept with a reason.

**In scope:** two native helpers edit a Rust parser and TypeScript display under disjoint grants. The parent inspects/integrates them and proves the interaction with its Verifier. **Escalation:** a parser fix requires changing the public protocol; the Implementer brings the source contradiction to Lead before crossing that boundary. Child success reports alone prove no integration.

## Handback

MUST load `accepting-work.md` and return the completed Lead-authored acceptance artifact against its five conditions, or affected gaps routed back to the pair, before independent delivery review. The Implementer report and original Verifier report are evidence you inspect, not your acceptance authors.

Read the diff before the summary. Check every changed path against the write scope; a file outside it needs its own disposition even when the change helps. Then hold the diff against the plan slice, as the main skill's case shows.

For skill writing, spot-check the inventory. Open two or three changed sentences, check them against the tells yourself, and put any miss with its anchor in the governing correction file before the next role/files call.

Put each correction and its anchor in the governing correction/review file, then send only implementer/files to the same Implementer. A new Implementer has to rebuild the context and can bring back the gap you just closed.

## When to stop

The relationship closes when the workstream and required proof support your completed acceptance and the final independent review is clean. Findings reopen only affected work/proof/acceptance before correction checking; a clean review starts no second wholesale acceptance phase. Stop sending corrections when the next one would change design meaning, scope or a contract; that goes back to the plan or to the owner.
