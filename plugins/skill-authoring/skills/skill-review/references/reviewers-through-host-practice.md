# Reviewers Through a Host Practice

Some host repositories have an agent-management practice that names roles, who may commission each one, models, launch routes and packets. When it defines a Reviewer role, start every stage reviewer as that role. The reviewer then brings the role's own rules on independence, read-only work and anchored findings to the checks you give it.

Return one commission per stage reviewer, sent through the practice, or the commissioner gap you returned to whoever asked for the review.

## Check that you may commission

Open the practice's Reviewer role and read who may commission it. Go on only when it admits you, as a review lead, to start the reviewers your review procedure prescribes. If it admits only another role, return that gap to whoever asked for the review before any reviewer starts, so they can commission the reviewers or widen the grant. Don't start the reviewers the host's own way instead; that skips the practice the host chose.

## Commission each reviewer

Follow the practice for everything it owns: the form, the model and any reviewer lineage rule, the launch route, the packet, and the prompt that tells the reviewer to load its role.

What this skill supplies to each commission:

- the target with its revision;
- the stage reference as the procedure, with that reviewer's check references in the stage's order and `checks/review-schema.md`;
- a request for candidate findings with anchors only, returned to you;
- no authoring history, so never a fork or resume of the author's session.

The reviewer loads the host's Reviewer role and that role's guidance for working with whoever commissioned it. Leave the role's rules out of the commission's text; the reviewer reads them itself.

## What stays with this skill

The stage still splits its checks into families across at least two reviewers. You open every candidate's anchor in step 3 before accepting it, whatever the role promised about its own findings. Corrections return to you under the convergence rule and accepted boundary in this skill's `SKILL.md`.

## Cases

Failed: the practice defines a Reviewer role, and the review lead starts two plain subagents with the check lists because that is quicker. Neither loads the role, so neither checks whether its session touched the target, and both run on the author's model family because the lineage rule lives in the practice.

Failed: the review lead commissions one Reviewer through the practice with "review this skill change" as its procedure. The reviewer picks its own checks, the two families never split, and it hands back a verdict where the review lead needed candidates to verify.

Clean: for a spec review, the review lead confirms the practice admits it as a commissioner and sends two commissions through it. The teaching reviewer gets the spec at its commit, `spec-review.md`, `mental-model-fit` then `depth-coverage`, and the schema. The structure reviewer gets the same target and procedure with `trigger-routing` then `rule-agreement`. The practice's model rules put at least one of them on a different lineage from the author. Each loads the role and returns anchored candidates, and the review lead opens every anchor.

Complete when: every reviewer the stage names went out through the practice with its own commission, or the commissioner gap went back to whoever asked for the review before any reviewer started.
